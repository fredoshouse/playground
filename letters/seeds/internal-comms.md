# Why internal comms are worse than external comms

*Seed — captured from the Sept 8 morning run. His words, cleaned up, not rewritten.*

---

Internal comms are usually worse than external comms. Why is that?

Coworkers, partners, direct reports — they all love to complain and push their needs from
their own vantage point, instead of being considerate of the whole thing they're part of.

Same with friendships. Same with relationships.

There are moments where you'll watch a person be kinder to a stranger, or to some loose
associate, than to the person they're actually close to. Split second. Why?

Why do people feel like it's allowed to be rude, to gossip, to dump their frustration on
the people who've helped them the most?

A few possible answers:

- They see you as a safe space, so the guard comes down.
- They think you can fix it.
- Straight disregard — their state is the priority and yours isn't.

If it's the third one, then it's selfish. It's dumping anxiety they can't handle onto
somebody who didn't ask for it.

So ask the real question: who wants to be part of an environment like that? Would you put
your kid, your niece, your nephew in it?

Probably not.

---

What I've watched over the last few years is the same cycle repeating. And honestly it's
forced me to be more present, and to say this out loud in places like this.

Here's the lesson underneath it: **nobody cares about you more than you.**

And it's a crazy thing, how kind and respectable we are to people we barely know.
Strangers, clients, vendors. We put on the best face and the best clothes, big smiles,
gracious interactions, all aimed at making the other person feel good about who we are.

Then we get to know them. Or we get the thing we wanted from them.

And that's when we start finding the holes. The problems. The annoyances. Usually because
they're no longer serving the need we wanted served in the first place.

---

## What this needs before it's publishable

- The trust formula he mentioned — that's the missing spine. Trust as the thing that
  earns you honesty instead of politeness, and what happens when it inverts.
- One real story. This lands much harder with a specific room and specific people than as
  a general observation.
- A landing. Right now it diagnoses and stops. The question worth answering: if you *are*
  the safe space people dump on, what do you actually do about it? Boundary, or exit, or
  something else.
- He also said the honest version of this involves his own vulnerability — that he'd write
  it as something he'd revisit a year later. That's the frame. Not a hot take. A thing he
  is still inside of.
