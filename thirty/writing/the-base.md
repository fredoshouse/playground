# The base

*Seed — Sept 9. The thing under the money. Private until he says otherwise.*

---

Savings rate: 0.1%.

Making more money than he ever has in his life. Spending more than he ever has. Debt
going up, not down. Working more, keeping less.

His words: *"I'm actually working more and actually able to use less of my money, but I'm
making more."*

---

## What he says the frustration is

Money going out to investments that haven't returned a dollar yet, on no timeline anyone
will name. Money going out on the house — rugs, food that gets bought instead of made.
And the part that stings: it isn't going to things that change his life. No new clothes,
no new items for him.

Meanwhile the commitments ahead are getting bigger. Wedding. Kids. And the base under all
of it doesn't feel like his.

*"I feel like I'm supporting everyone else's dream except mine."*

---

## What it probably actually is

Not a spending problem. He says it himself — he knows how to invest, he's made money
while spending money. The number that bothers him isn't the outflow, it's the **0.1%**.

It's a leverage problem. He wanted to be able to walk away from a job on his own terms,
and the thing that buys that is a base. Right now every extra dollar he earns gets
claimed before it can become one.

That's why it reads as *being taxed* rather than *overspending.* And it's why the rug
conversation isn't about a rug.

The second piece: he can't say any of this out loud without it landing as "you don't want
to spend money," which isn't what he means and isn't true. So it goes unsaid, and it
compounds.

---

## What to do with it

- **Get the actual numbers on one page.** Income, fixed, variable, debt, what's committed
  to investments and on what timeline. He's arguing from a feeling right now. He'd argue
  much better from a spreadsheet — and he's the kind of person who would.
- **Name one number together.** Not a budget. A savings rate. 0.1% → anything with a whole
  number in front of it. A target you both agreed to beats a resentment you're carrying
  alone.
- **Put a date on the investments.** "When does a dollar come back" is a fair question to
  ask out loud, in writing, of every one of them.
- **Therapy.** He raised it himself, twice, unprompted. That's not a suggestion to make —
  it's a decision he's already reached and hasn't booked yet.

---

## The line worth keeping

*"I'm going to write my way and try to make money in my own space, my own way. I've got
to work at it every day and own this narrative."*

That's the actual goal. Everything above is in service of it.
